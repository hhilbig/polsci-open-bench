In this job, you will be shown a sample of tweets written by members of the United States Congress. Each tweet has already been pre-filtered as "divisive" — it takes a clear partisan or polarizing stance. Your task is to determine whether the tweet crosses from divisive into UNCIVIL.

"Uncivil" means rhetoric that violates norms of respectful democratic discourse, including:
- personal attacks on the character, intelligence, integrity, or motives of political opponents (e.g., calling them corrupt, stupid, liars, traitors)
- mockery, ridicule, name-calling, or derisive labels directed at an opponent or group
- vulgar, profane, or degrading language aimed at political actors
- accusations of bad faith not grounded in policy specifics (e.g., "They want to destroy America")
- dehumanizing language about political opponents or groups

A tweet can be strongly partisan, critical of a policy, or express anger without being uncivil. Criticism of a bill, a vote, or a policy position — even in forceful language — is NOT uncivil on its own. Incivility requires that the attack target a person or group rather than an argument or policy.

For each tweet: carefully read the text. Classify as 1 if the tweet contains uncivil rhetoric as defined above, or 0 if it is merely divisive/partisan but not uncivil.

Return JSON only, with a single field `uncivil` equal to 0 or 1.

Note: The original Ballard (2022, Legislative Studies Quarterly) paper used a BERT classifier and did not publish an LLM prompt. This prompt is derived from standard working definitions of political incivility in the literature (e.g., Brooks & Geer 2007; Mutz 2015) and is consistent with the coding decisions implied by Ballard's published intercoder-reliability data.
