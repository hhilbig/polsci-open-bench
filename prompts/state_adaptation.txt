CATEGORIES (can select multiple if bill genuinely spans topics):
- adaptation: PROACTIVE measures to reduce climate risks BEFORE disasters occur
  Examples: flood control infrastructure, drainage improvements, water conservation, drought preparedness, wildfire prevention, sea level rise protection, heat mitigation, stormwater management, groundwater conservation, flood insurance requirements, water supply resilience, industrial water use permits
  Key insight: If a bill improves infrastructure, insurance, or resource management to PREVENT or prepare for future climate damage, it's adaptation. Be INCLUSIVE - flood insurance, water supply standards, and water use permits are adaptation when they address climate/disaster resilience.
  NOT adaptation: Bills about synthetic chemical contamination (PFAS, PFOS, 1,4-Dioxane, lead, mercury) are environmental/public health regulation, NOT adaptation, unless explicitly tied to climate impacts (e.g., climate-driven flooding spreading contaminants).

- mitigation: Reducing greenhouse gas emissions (CO2, methane, N2O)
  Examples: renewable energy (solar, wind), electric vehicles, energy efficiency, carbon reduction, carbon capture, GHG emissions standards, clean energy incentives
  Key insight: If a bill reduces GHG emissions or promotes clean energy, it's mitigation. "Emissions" means greenhouse gas emissions specifically — bills about criteria pollutants (SOx, NOx, PM2.5), hazardous air pollutants, vehicle inspection programs, or Clean Air Act public health standards are NOT mitigation unless they specifically target greenhouse gases.

- relief: Response AFTER a disaster has already occurred
  Examples: emergency funding for declared disasters, FEMA coordination, disaster declarations, rebuilding assistance, recovery programs
  Key insight: If a bill responds to a PAST event with emergency assistance, it's relief. If it builds infrastructure to PREVENT future disasters, it's adaptation (not relief)

- irrelevant: Not meaningfully related to climate change or natural disasters
  Examples: general water quality (fluoridation, microplastics), routine government operations, pure public health unrelated to climate
  Key insight: Just mentioning "water" doesn't make it climate-related. Ask: does this bill address climate risks?

INSTRUMENTS (can have multiple, but be CONSERVATIVE - only mark if clearly present):
- regulatory: ONLY if the bill creates mandates, standards, requirements, permits, or enforcement mechanisms on REGULATED ENTITIES (private parties, businesses, property owners). Directing government agencies to study/report is NOT regulatory. Do NOT mark regulatory just because government is involved. Tax exemptions, surcharges, and fee adjustments are FISCAL instruments, not regulatory — even if they "require" payment.
- fiscal: If the bill involves (a) specific appropriations, grants, or subsidies, (b) tax credits or exemptions, (c) bond authorization, (d) expansion or redirection of existing government funds toward new eligible purposes (even without new money), or (e) tradable market-based instruments (renewable energy credits, carbon credits). Merely MENTIONING "fund" or "program" is NOT fiscal — there must be actual money being allocated, redirected, or a market-based financial mechanism created. Bills that award grants or provide financial assistance are fiscal, NOT regulatory, even if the grant comes with eligibility conditions.
- symbolic: If the bill is ceremonial (commemorative days, memorials, resolutions) OR primarily creates governance structures (offices, commissions, committees), sets policy goals/definitions, or requires studies/reports WITHOUT imposing binding mandates on external parties or appropriating specific funds. Symbolic CAN coexist with other instruments — e.g., a bill that creates an office (symbolic) AND appropriates funds (fiscal). Bills that "require establishment of projects" or "direct implementation" with binding mandates are NOT symbolic — they are regulatory.

CRITICAL NEGATIVE CONSTRAINTS:

A. The "Administrative" Trap (Avoid Adaptation False Positives):
- NOT ADAPTATION: Administrative bills about water districts, insurance bodies, or climate-related agencies that only concern governance (board elections, residency requirements, filing deadlines, reinsurance capital adjustments, forfeiture periods) are IRRELEVANT.
- IS ADAPTATION: Only label as Adaptation if the bill physically changes infrastructure OR substantively alters resource management policy (e.g., "Funding for new levees," "Mandatory water rationing," "New flood control standards").

B. The "Symbolic" Guidance:
- CEREMONIAL = SYMBOLIC: Commemorative days, memorials, congratulatory resolutions.
- RESOLUTIONS THAT "URGE" = SYMBOLIC: Resolutions that urge, request, or encourage action are symbolic, NOT regulatory, regardless of how specific the urged action is. "Urges" does not create mandates on external entities.
- STUDIES/REPORTS = SYMBOLIC: If a bill primarily requires a study, report, or policy analysis (e.g., "requires a report on X," "directs study of Y," "integrated policy report"), this is SYMBOLIC. These don't create binding mandates on regulated parties. A study/report should still be classified by its subject matter. A bill requiring a study on renewable energy is mitigation + symbolic, not irrelevant. Only mark a study/report as irrelevant if its subject matter is itself unrelated to climate.
- NOT SYMBOLIC: Bills that create actual programs with implementation authority, require specific actions (not just studies), or establish binding standards. If a bill "establishes a program" with operational funding, it's NOT symbolic.
- ADMINISTRATIVE/IRRELEVANT = NO INSTRUMENT: If a bill is purely administrative (board elections, filing deadlines, forfeiture periods), it should have symbolic=0, regulatory=0, fiscal=0.

C. Instrument Discipline:
- If you mark a topic as IRRELEVANT, set regulatory=0 and fiscal=0. Symbolic is allowed for irrelevant bills ONLY for commemorative actions (e.g., "Renewable Energy Day").
- Don't mark regulatory just because government is involved.

D. The "Fiscal" Over-Reach (Fixing Fiscal False Positives):
- NOT FISCAL: Creating a "program" or "fund" without appropriating money is NOT fiscal. Structure changes to revolving funds are NOT fiscal.
- NOT FISCAL: Dam safety requirements, insurance regulation, permit requirements without specific funding are regulatory, NOT fiscal.
- IS FISCAL: Must have SPECIFIC appropriations ("$X million"), grant programs with funding, tax credits, bond authorization with amounts, or explicit funding mechanisms.
- GRANT PROGRAMS: Grant programs are fiscal even without a specific dollar amount, because grants inherently involve distributing funds. The "creates fund without money" exclusion applies to administrative fund structures (revolving funds, reserve accounts), not to programs whose purpose is to award grants.

E. The "Regulatory" Over-Reach (Fixing Regulatory False Positives):
- NOT REGULATORY: Bills directing government agencies to "study," "report," "develop plans," or "coordinate" are procedural/administrative, NOT regulatory.
- NOT REGULATORY: Creating advisory committees, conservancies, or study commissions without enforcement authority.
- IS REGULATORY: Must impose requirements on EXTERNAL entities (private parties, businesses, property owners, utilities) through mandates, standards, permits, or enforcement mechanisms.

F. Water Domain Default:
- Water bills are IRRELEVANT unless they explicitly reference at least one climate risk (flood risk, drought risk, wildfire risk, sea-level rise, climate change, greenhouse gases, extreme heat, climate resilience, disaster recovery, hazard mitigation) OR the bill explicitly frames a governance entity/program as serving climate resilience, drought resilience, flood resilience, or disaster preparedness.
- "Water conservation," "groundwater," "aquifer," "irrigation," "water quality," "watershed," "district governance," and "water rights/permits" do NOT imply climate unless an explicit climate-risk hook is present.

G. Insurance Domain Default:
- Insurance bills are IRRELEVANT unless they explicitly reference climate perils (wildfire, flood, hurricane, extreme weather) and the bill's mechanism targets those perils, OR the bill explicitly frames an insurance program as addressing climate/disaster risk (e.g., "insurance affordability for disaster-prone areas").
- "Market stability," "reinsurance," "solvency," "rates," and "consumer protections" are NOT climate-related on their own.

IMPORTANT - REVERSAL BILLS:
Classify bills by the climate topic they AFFECT, regardless of whether they support or oppose that policy.
- A bill prohibiting offshore wind = mitigation (it affects renewable energy policy)
- A bill disapproving EPA climate rules = adaptation or mitigation (it affects climate regulation)
- A bill adding surcharges on EVs = mitigation (it affects clean transportation)

Set reversal=1 if the bill:
- Prohibits, bans, or blocks climate action
- Repeals existing climate legislation
- Uses congressional disapproval to overturn environmental rules

- Creates regulatory barriers to renewable energy

NOT reversal: Repealing fees/surcharges on clean energy (pro-climate), repealing coal/fossil fuel subsidies (pro-climate), or standard renewable energy regulations. The bill must OPPOSE climate goals to be reversal.
